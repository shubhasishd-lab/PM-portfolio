The Organisation aspect covers all roles in a Scrum project — both the Scrum Core Team (who do the work) and the non-core stakeholders (who have an interest in the outcome).

💡 Scrum insight: Clear role definition prevents the #1 Scrum failure mode — people reverting to traditional command-and-control behaviours under pressure.
A2
Business Justification
Value-driven delivery · ROI · Business case
›

The Business Justification aspect ensures every Scrum project is driven by demonstrable business value. The Product Owner is accountable for ensuring the project remains justified throughout its life — not just at the start.

Value-Driven Delivery
Business Case
Continuous Justification
Expected Benefits
💡 Scrum insight: Business justification is not a one-time exercise. If a project stops delivering value, Scrum gives the Product Owner the authority to terminate it early.
A3
Quality
Definition of Done · Acceptance criteria · Continuous improvement
›

Quality in Scrum means delivering a product that meets acceptance criteria and achieves the business outcomes expected. Quality is everyone's responsibility — not just testers.

Definition of Done
Acceptance Criteria
Continuous Quality
Technical Debt
💡 Scrum insight: Quality cannot be traded for speed. A team that ships broken software faster is not a high-performing team — they\'re creating problems for the future.
A4
Change
Embracing change · Change management · Adaptability
›

Scrum is designed to embrace change rather than resist it. Changes in requirements are expected and welcome — the Product Backlog can be updated at any time, and the team adapts sprint by sprint.

Welcoming Change
Backlog Changes
Sprint Protection
Sprint Cancellation
💡 Scrum insight: Change is not the enemy — it\'s reality. Scrum\'s genius is making change cheap by reducing the cost of each iteration.
A5
Risk
Risk identification · Assessment · Mitigation · Adaptation
›

Risk management in Scrum is continuous and built into the framework. Short sprint cycles naturally reduce risk by producing working software frequently and enabling early detection of problems.

Risk Identification
Risk Assessment
Risk Mitigation
Iterative Risk Reduction
💡 Scrum insight: Scrum is inherently risk-reducing. Short sprints mean problems surface quickly — long before they become project-ending crises.
19 Scrum Processes — Across 5 Phases
P1
Initiate Phase — 6 Processes
Vision · Team · Backlog · Stakeholders
›

The Initiate phase establishes the foundation. 6 processes create the project vision, identify the team, develop Epics, and set up the initial Product Backlog.

1. Create Project Vision
2. Identify SM & Stakeholders
3. Form Scrum Team
4. Develop Epics
5. Create Prioritised Backlog
6. Release Planning
💡 Phase 1 insight: The Project Vision is the North Star for the entire project. Invest time getting it right — everything else follows from it.
P2
Plan & Estimate Phase — 6 Processes
Stories · Estimation · Sprint planning · Commitment
›

The Plan & Estimate phase takes the Product Backlog and turns it into an executable sprint plan. 6 processes cover user story creation, estimation, task breakdown, and sprint commitment.

7. Create User Stories
8. Approve, Estimate & Commit
9. Create Tasks
10. Estimate Tasks
11. Create Sprint Backlog
12. Update Release Plan
💡 Phase 2 insight: Good estimation is a team skill — it improves with practice. Never let managers override team estimates. The team does the work; the team estimates the work.
P3
Implement Phase — 4 Processes
Sprint execution · Standup · Impediments · Deliverables
›

The Implement phase is where value is created. 4 processes govern sprint execution, daily synchronisation, impediment removal, and deliverable production.

13. Create Deliverables
14. Daily Standup
15. Groom Product Backlog
16. Update Burndown Chart
💡 Phase 3 insight: The Scrum Board (physical or digital) is the team\'s heartbeat during sprint execution. It makes work visible, surfaces blockers, and creates shared accountability.
P4
Review & Retrospect Phase — 2 Processes
Sprint review · Retrospective · Inspect & adapt
›

The Review & Retrospect phase closes each sprint with inspection and adaptation — the two most powerful words in Scrum. 2 processes drive product improvement and team improvement simultaneously.

17. Demonstrate & Validate
18. Retrospect Sprint
💡 Phase 4 insight: These 2 processes — Review and Retrospect — are what make Scrum a learning system. Teams that skip them stop improving and eventually stop delivering value.
P5
Release Phase — 1 Process
Ship · Working agreement · Lessons learned
›

The Release phase covers shipping the Potentially Shippable Product Increment to end users. 1 process governs the release decision, documentation, and lessons learned capture.

19. Ship Deliverables
💡 Phase 5 insight: In best-practice Scrum, every sprint produces a potentially shippable increment. The business decides when to actually ship — but the team is always ready.
